Bank, Wallet & Scam Calls

Fake bank officers, fake police, wallet reversal tricks and loan apps. What to do, what never to do, and who to call.

Golden rule: a real bank, JazzCash, Easypaisa, FBR, NADRA, the police or the FIA will never ask for your OTP, PIN, password or full card number on a call or message. Hang up, then call the number printed on your card or inside your official app.

If money already left your account

  1. Call your bank or wallet nowUse the number on your card or in the official app. Ask them to block the transaction, freeze the account and log a fraud complaint. Note the reference number.
  2. Change passwords and PINsChange your banking app password, ATM PIN and email password from a safe device.
  3. Report to NCCIACall 1799 or file on the NCCIA website. Bring screenshots, transaction IDs, phone numbers and account numbers used by the scammer.
  4. Escalate if the bank does not helpState Bank of Pakistan Consumer Protection: call 111-727-273 or use the Sunwai complaint portal and app. Banks must respond to fraud complaints within the SBP timelines.

Protect Your SIMs & CNIC

Criminals use SIMs registered on other people's CNICs. Check yours every few months. It takes one minute.

Check SIMs registered on your CNIC

  1. By SMSType your 13-digit CNIC without dashes or spaces. Send it to 668 from any network. You get the number of SIMs per operator. Normal SMS charges apply.
  2. OnlineOpen the PTA SIM information page at cnic.sims.pk, enter your CNIC and submit. Use only this address. Fake lookalike sites exist.
  3. Found a SIM you do not recognise?Contact that operator's helpline with your original CNIC, ask them to block it as unauthorised, and note the complaint number.
  4. No fix from the operator?Complain to PTA: call 0800-55055 or use complaint.pta.gov.pk.
  5. Used by criminals?If a SIM in your name was used in a crime or fraud, report it to NCCIA on 1799 so the record shows you reported it.

Other useful short codes

668Send your CNIC to see SIMs registered on it.
8484Send a phone's IMEI to check whether the device is registered with PTA.
667Send MNP to check number portability details.

Keep your CNIC safe

Do

  • Share a CNIC copy only when required, and write the purpose and date across it.
  • Keep SIM biometric receipts and cut up old SIMs.
  • Block and report a lost phone or SIM at once.
  • Check your credit report for loans you never took. Ask your bank how to request your eCIB report from the State Bank.

Do not

  • Post a photo of your CNIC, passport or SIM packet online.
  • Send your CNIC picture to an unknown number, even for a prize or a job.
  • Lend your SIM or fingerprint to someone to register their SIM.
  • Pay a person to "check SIM owner details". Those are scams.

For a lost, damaged or misused CNIC, contact NADRA on 1777 or visit your nearest NADRA centre.

Daraz, Online Shopping & Business Protection

Avoid shopping fraud. Check your business name before you register. Find and remove anyone using your name.

Pick a name that is free

Search your name everywhere

Report and remove fakes

1. Before you open a business: check the name

  1. Search the SECP name databaseOpen the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan website (secp.gov.pk), go to eServices and use Name Availability Search. Try spelling variations, because similar names can be refused.
  2. Check other placesSearch the Intellectual Property Organisation of Pakistan for trademarks, check the domain name and search Facebook, Instagram and Google Maps for the same name.
  3. Reserve the name and registerSECP reserves approved names for a limited time. Complete incorporation inside that window. SECP helpline: 0800-88008. Business Care on WhatsApp: 0306-9365625.
  4. Protect the brand earlyRegister the trademark, claim the same handle on every social app and register the domain.

2. Has someone opened a branch or business in your name?

How to find out

  • Search your business and personal name on Google, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Daraz, OLX and Google Maps.
  • Ask SECP (0800-88008) whether any company or business is registered with your name or CNIC.
  • Check that your NTN and tax status on the FBR website are as you expect.
  • Run the 668 SIM check on your CNIC.
  • Ask customers to send screenshots of anything suspicious.

How to shut it down

  • Fake page or profile: report it inside the app as impersonation, then file with NCCIA on 1799.
  • Company registered in a confusingly similar name: complain to SECP, 0800-88008, complaints@secp.gov.pk.
  • Trademark misuse: send a notice and file with the IPO of Pakistan. A lawyer can help.
  • Fake NTN or tax use: report to FBR.
  • Fraud in your name: file an FIR with police (call 15) and keep the copy.

Senior Citizens: Stay Safe on the Phone

Large text. Simple rules. Share this page with your parents and grandparents.

Rule 1. Never tell anyone your OTP.An OTP is a secret code. No bank, no police officer and no company needs it from you.
Rule 2. If a call scares you, hang up.Scammers say your account will close or you will be arrested. Real offices do not threaten you on the phone.
Rule 3. You never pay money to receive money.A real prize or grant does not need a fee, a tax payment or a code.
Rule 4. Call your family first.Before you send any money, talk to a son, daughter or trusted person.

Schools & Family Safety

Lessons for students, a checklist for teachers and parents, and where to get help when a child is harmed online.

Six lessons for students

Checklist for parents and teachers

  1. Keep devices in shared spacesYounger children should use phones and tablets where an adult can see.
  2. Set accounts to privateUse the privacy steps in the Social Media Safety guide. On TikTok use Family Pairing.
  3. Agree the "tell me" ruleA child will not be punished for reporting a scary message, a threat or a stranger.
  4. Teach "stop, block, tell"Stop replying, block the account, tell a trusted adult. Save screenshots first.
  5. Add the school to the planName one teacher to receive cyberbullying reports and agree what happens next.

If a child is harmed or threatened online

Immediately

  • Do not delete the evidence. Take screenshots with the account name, link and time.
  • Do not pay or send more photos. Paying makes demands grow.
  • Block the account and report it inside the app.

Report to

  • NCCIA cybercrime helpline 1799 or helpdesk@nccia.gov.pk
  • Child Protection helpline 1121 (coverage differs by province)
  • Madadgaar helpline 1098 for children and women in distress
  • Police 15 if the child is in danger now

Complain Center

Your first minute, your first hour, and how to file an official complaint.

Account hacked? Do this in 60 seconds

  1. Open the app or site's own "forgot password" page and reset your password. Use a new, long one.
  2. Change the password of the email address linked to that account first, if you can.
  3. Turn on two-step verification using an authenticator app or the app's own security option.
  4. Log out of all other devices from the security or login-activity page.
  5. Warn friends and family that messages from you may be fake.
  6. If money or blackmail is involved, call 1799 and your bank straight away.

How to file a cybercrime complaint

  1. Collect evidenceScreenshots of chats, profile links, phone numbers, bank or wallet transaction IDs, dates and times. Do not edit them.
  2. Call or write to NCCIAHelpline 1799. Email helpdesk@nccia.gov.pk. You can also use the complaint form on the official NCCIA website or visit your nearest NCCIA circle office. The FIA cyber wing was merged into NCCIA, so the FIA no longer takes cybercrime complaints.
  3. Police can start it tooPolice helpline 15 is linked with NCCIA, so cybercrime complaints you make through 15 are forwarded to the right NCCIA office.
  4. Save your complaint numberYou need it for follow-up. Follow up by phone or email if you hear nothing in a few working days.
  5. Tell the platform and your bankReport the account inside the app and inform your bank or wallet company at the same time.

How to use a helpline well

  1. Keep your evidence openHave screenshots, numbers and transaction IDs ready before you call.
  2. Say it in one sentence first"I sent Rs 20,000 to a wallet number after a fake bank call today at 3 pm."
  3. Ask for a complaint numberWrite down the number, the date and the name of the person.
  4. Never give an OTP or PIN to a helpline callerIf someone calls you back claiming to be a helpline, hang up and dial the number from this page.
  5. If the line does not connectTry the email or website listed by the agency, or go to the nearest office. Hours and numbers can change.
